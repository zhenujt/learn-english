# 14-Day Software Meeting and Demo Survival Course

> A task-first course for beginners who need to join English meetings and demonstrate software quickly. Learn each phrase as a complete unit, then use the grammar guide to understand and extend it.

[Chinese version](software-meeting-demo-14-day.zh.md) · [Grammar guide](software-workplace-grammar-guide.md) · [Workplace phrasebook](software-workplace-phrases.md)

## How to use this course

Study for 20–30 minutes each day:

1. Play the complete audio at the top: each Chinese prompt once and each English phrase three times in Jenny's voice.
2. Use the play button beside any example to practise that English phrase on its own.
3. Replace the bracketed information and produce at least three new sentences.
4. Record the daily task without restarting after a small mistake.
5. Continue only when you can begin within three seconds and finish the task.

## Day 1: Join and start a meeting

Core phrases: `Hi everyone. Thanks for joining.` `Can everyone hear me clearly?` `Can everyone see my screen?` `Let me share my screen.` `Shall we get started?` `I will keep this brief.`

Mission: Deliver a 20-second opening that includes a greeting, audio check, screen check, and meeting start.

## Day 2: Ask for clarification

Core phrases: `Could you say that again, please?` `Could you speak a little more slowly?` `I did not catch the last part.` `Could you rephrase the question?` `Could you give me an example?` `Let me make sure I understood correctly.`

Mission: Say three different clarification requests without reading them.

## Day 3: Introduce the page and goal

Core phrases: `Today, I will show the main user flow.` `This is the user management page.` `The goal is to make this process faster.` `This feature helps users manage their accounts.` `I am using a test account with sample data.` `This is the latest build from the test environment.`

Mission: Introduce a real page, the demo goal, and the environment in 30 seconds.

## Day 4: Describe actions and results

Core phrases: `I click this button.` `The dialog opens.` `I enter a keyword in this field.` `The matching results appear below.` `I select an option from the list.` `The page updates automatically.`

Mission: Open a real page and describe at least six visible actions and results.

## Day 5: Present a sequence

Core phrases: `First, I open the filter menu.` `Next, I select Active.` `Then, I apply the filter.` `Now, the table shows only active users.` `Finally, I clear the filter.` `That completes the workflow.`

Mission: Present a 45-second flow with First, Next, Then, and Finally.

## Day 6: Explain value and limitations

Core phrases: `This saves users time.` `This reduces the number of manual steps.` `The main benefit is faster feedback.` `At the moment, this supports one option at a time.` `This is not included in the current scope.` `We plan to support this in a future release.`

Mission: Explain one feature's value, current limitation, and future plan.

## Day 7: Deliver the first complete demo

Core phrases: `Let me start with some context.` `The current process takes too many steps.` `I will show you the new flow.` `First, I open the page and select an item.` `The result appears immediately.` `That covers the main flow.`

Mission: Record a 60-second demo without restarting.

## Day 8: Give a status update

Core phrases: `Yesterday, I completed the implementation.` `Today, I am testing the new flow.` `The main work is complete.` `I still need to update the documentation.` `My next step is to open the pull request.` `I expect to finish this today.`

Mission: Give a 30-second stand-up update covering yesterday, today, and the next step.

## Day 9: Explain blockers and timing

Core phrases: `I am blocked by a permissions issue.` `I am waiting for access to the test environment.` `There is one open question.` `I need help from the backend team.` `If I get access today, I can finish by Friday.` `I will share an update this afternoon.`

Mission: State the blocker, required help, expected finish time, and next update.

## Day 10: Handle a demo problem

Core phrases: `It looks like the test environment is slow.` `Let me try that again.` `I will refresh the page.` `That is not the expected behavior.` `I will investigate this after the meeting.` `Let me continue with the next part.`

Mission: Create a harmless page error, explain it, and continue the demo without stopping.

## Day 11: Answer common questions

Core phrases: `The short answer is yes.` `There are two parts to that question.` `This only affects new users.` `This does not change the existing workflow.` `That is outside the current scope.` `We have not made a final decision yet.`

Mission: Answer three questions with both a conclusion and a scope statement.

## Day 12: Respond when you do not know

Core phrases: `I do not have the exact number with me.` `I do not want to guess.` `I need to check that with the team.` `I will verify this after the meeting.` `I will follow up in the team chat.` `Does that answer your question?`

Mission: Respond honestly to three unknown questions and state how you will follow up.

## Day 13: Invite and clarify questions

Core phrases: `I will pause here and take questions.` `What questions do you have?` `Could you clarify what you mean by performance?` `Are you asking about the user experience or the implementation?` `Could you give an example of that scenario?` `Let me repeat the question to make sure I understood.`

Mission: Complete a three-minute Q&A and use at least two clarification phrases.

## Day 14: Complete meeting and demo

Core phrases: `Hi everyone. Today, I will show the new user flow.` `The goal is to reduce the number of manual steps.` `First, I open the page and enter a keyword.` `The matching results appear immediately.` `This saves users time and makes the process clearer.` `That covers the main flow. What questions do you have?`

Mission: Deliver a two-minute demo and a three-minute Q&A without a script. Use a Day 10 or Day 12 phrase when something goes wrong.

## Graduation standard

- Start a meeting or demo within three seconds.
- Describe at least six page actions and results continuously.
- Complete the context, goal, action, result, value, and questions sequence.
- Request repetition or clarification when needed.
- Avoid guessing and state a concrete follow-up when an answer is unknown.
- Explain a demo problem and continue instead of becoming silent.

Continue with the [Software Workplace Grammar Guide](software-workplace-grammar-guide.md), focusing first on the present simple, present continuous, past simple, future forms, questions, and modal verbs.
