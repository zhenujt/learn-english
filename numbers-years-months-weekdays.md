| 1,000,000 | one million |
| 10,000,000 | ten million |
| 100,000,000 | one hundred million |

English groups large numbers in sets of three digits, not in sets of four as Chinese does.

```text
21 = twenty-one
105 = one hundred and five
2,026 = two thousand and twenty-six
45,678 = forty-five thousand six hundred and seventy-eight
3,405,019 = three million four hundred and five thousand and nineteen
100,000,000 = one hundred million
```

British English commonly uses **and** after `hundred`; American English often omits it. Both `one hundred and five` and `one hundred five` are correct.

## 4. Years

Four-digit years are often split into two two-digit groups.

| Year | Common reading |
|---:|---|
| 1900 | nineteen hundred |
| 1905 | nineteen oh five |
| 1984 | nineteen eighty-four |
| 2000 | two thousand |
| 2008 | two thousand and eight |
| 2010 | twenty ten / two thousand and ten |
| 2026 | twenty twenty-six / two thousand and twenty-six |

```text
The new office will open in 2026.
Our company was founded in 1998.
```

## 6. Weekdays

| Weekday | Abbreviation | Weekday | Abbreviation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Mon | Friday | Fri |
| Tuesday | Tue | Saturday | Sat |
| Wednesday | Wed | Sunday | Sun |
| Thursday | Thu |  |  |

```text

```

Months and weekdays always begin with a capital letter.

## 7. Dates

Use ordinal numbers when reading dates: `first`, `second`, `third`, `fourth`, and so on.

| Written date | British reading | American reading |
|---|---|---|
| 6 September 2026 | the sixth of September, twenty twenty-six | September sixth, twenty twenty-six |
| 1 January 2027 | the first of January, twenty twenty-seven | January first, twenty twenty-seven |

```text
Today is September sixth, twenty twenty-six.
The deadline is Friday, October second.
```

## Quick Review

1. Read 21-99 as tens plus ones: `twenty-six`.
2. Read large numbers in three-digit groups: million, thousand, then the final group.
3. Read most four-digit years as two two-digit groups: `2026` becomes `twenty twenty-six`.
4. Capitalize every month and weekday.
5. Use `on` with weekdays and dates, and `in` with months and years.
